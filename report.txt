--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PART 1: DATASET DESCRIPTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The dataset is a synthetic (artificially generated) set of clinical measurements,
lifestyle habits and activity data used to study heart-disease risk. The real-world
problem it reflects: heart disease is a leading cause of death, and doctors judge a
patient's risk from many factors together (blood pressure, cholesterol, age,
lifestyle, and so on).

All 27 features were described (meaning, data type, numerical vs categorical):
- 19 numerical features: age, resting BP (systolic/diastolic), total cholesterol,
  HDL, LDL, triglycerides, fasting blood sugar, HbA1c, BMI, resting heart rate,
  max heart rate achieved, ST depression, alcohol per week, exercise minutes per
  week, sleep hours, stress score, daily steps, diet quality score.
- 7 categorical features: sex, chest pain type, exercise-induced angina,
  family history, smoker status, wearable owner, and the target
  has_heart_disease.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PART 2: CENTRAL TENDENCY AND DISPERSION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
For every numerical feature we computed mean, median, mode, variance, standard
deviation, Q1, Q3 and IQR. Highlights:

- Age: mean ~54, median ~54, IQR 45-63, std ~13. Symmetric, realistic adult range.
- Blood pressure: systolic ~128 mmHg, diastolic ~81 mmHg. On the borderline between
  normal and elevated.
- Total cholesterol: mean ~189 mg/dL, std ~32. Values above 200 are high, so part
  of the sample is at risk.
- BMI: mean ~25.3. Just over the healthy-weight boundary (25-30 is overweight).
- Right-skewed (mean > median): st_depression (1.01 vs 0.70), alcohol per week
  (5.71 vs 4.20), daily_steps (the most variable feature, largest IQR ~2975).
- Near-symmetric: stress_score (48.0 vs 48.1), sleep_hours (7.0 vs 7.0).
- Interesting modes: exercise_minutes_per_week mode = 0 (many record no exercise),
  triglycerides mode = 35 while the mean is 152 (big low-value group).
- The target is imbalanced: 6273 zeros vs 2727 ones (30.3% positive).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PART 3: DISTRIBUTION AND SHAPE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Each numerical feature got a 1x3 plot (histogram + density curve, boxplot) with
mean/median lines and the skewness value, plus an IQR outlier check.

- Shape rule used: |skew| < 0.5 -> Symmetric; skew > +0.5 -> Right-skewed;
  skew < -0.5 -> Left-skewed. Mean and median noted to agree with the shape.
- Result: 17 of 19 features are symmetric. Only two are clearly right-skewed:
  - st_depression: skew ~2.04, 5.4% outliers (the most of any feature).
  - alcohol_units_per_week: skew ~2.03, 4.8% outliers.
- The zero-spikes (exercise_minutes_per_week, daily_steps mode = 500) capture
  extreme patient behaviour and are the most interesting for later prediction.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PART 4: CATEGORICAL VARIABLE ANALYSIS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Each categorical feature got a frequency table (count + %), a bar chart and a pie
chart, plus the most common category and an imbalance comment.

- sex and wearable_owner are nearly balanced, so group comparisons are fair.
- chest_pain_type, exercise_induced_angina, family_history and smoker_status are
  imbalanced (one category dominates in each).
- The target is imbalanced (30.3% positive). The predictive models in Part 8 must
  handle this (evaluation with F1 / ROC-AUC rather than plain accuracy).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PART 5: RELATIONSHIPS BETWEEN VARIABLES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Scatterplots for numerical pairs (colored by disease) and boxplots/countplots for
numeric-or-categorical vs the binary target. Pearson r reported for every pair.

- Age vs total cholesterol: r = 0.128 (weak). Disease patients are older (59.7 vs
  51.5) and have slightly higher cholesterol (197 vs 185 mg/dL).
- Age vs cholesterol split by sex: males have higher disease rate (35.4% vs 24.7%).
- HDL vs LDL: r = -0.082 (independent). Disease group has lower HDL (50.5 vs 57.3)
  and higher LDL (114.4 vs 98.5).
- Age vs BMI split by sex: r = 0.039 (independent).
- BMI vs disease: mean 26.7 vs 24.7, r = 0.213.
- Chest pain type vs disease: Typical Angina highest (47.9%), Asymptomatic lowest
  (25.4%).
- Fasting blood sugar vs disease: 126.5 vs 116.4, r = 0.202.
- Family history vs disease: 35.6% with history vs 28.3% without.
- Resting BP vs disease: systolic r = 0.249, diastolic r = 0.193.
- Smoking & alcohol vs disease: current smokers 46.4% disease rate; alcohol
  correlation only 0.041 (weak).

Correlation strength table used: 0.00-0.20 very weak, 0.20-0.40 weak, 0.40-0.60
moderate, 0.60-0.80 strong, 0.80-1.00 very strong.

Strongest correlations with the target: max_heart_rate_achieved (-0.581),
st_depression (+0.357), age (+0.291), ldl (+0.267), hdl (-0.252),
resting_bp_systolic (+0.249).

A full 20x20 correlation matrix (all 19 numerical features + the target) is
drawn as a heatmap in section 5.12. It confirms the same strongest features and
also shows feature-to-feature relationships: systolic and diastolic BP are
strongly correlated, HDL is negatively correlated with triglycerides, and the
cholesterol features (total cholesterol, LDL, HDL, triglycerides) form a cluster.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PART 6: DATA QUALITY ASSESSMENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Five checks were run:

1. Missing values: 0 in every column. Nothing to impute.
2. Duplicate rows: 0. No patient is counted twice.
3. Min/max of every numerical column: all values plausible, no negatives.
4. Sensible-range bounds check (clinical ranges): every value inside its range.
5. Categorical unexpected-categories check: only the expected categories.

Discussion of handling (if problems existed): drop missing rows or impute with
mean/median/mode; drop duplicates; fix or remove impossible values. The Part 3
IQR outliers (high ST-depression, obesity, heavy drinking) are extreme but real
patients and are kept, not removed.

Conclusion: the dataset is completely clean. No preprocessing is needed.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PART 7: KEY INSIGHTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7.1 Typical patient profile of heart disease (with disease vs without):
    Age 59.7 vs 51.5 years (older)
    Systolic BP 133.1 vs 125.6 mmHg (higher)
    Diastolic BP 84.0 vs 79.4 mmHg (higher)
    LDL 114.4 vs 98.5 mg/dL (higher)
    HDL 50.5 vs 57.3 mg/dL (lower)
    BMI 26.7 vs 24.7 (higher)
    Fasting blood sugar 126.5 vs 116.4 mg/dL (higher)

7.2 Strongest single signals:
    - max_heart_rate_achieved (r = -0.58): by far the most informative. Disease
      patients reach a much lower max heart rate during exercise.
    - st_depression (r = +0.36): classic ECG sign of heart disease.
    - age (r = +0.29), LDL (r = +0.27), HDL (r = -0.25), systolic BP (r = +0.25).

7.3 Categorical / lifestyle insights:
    - Typical Angina: 47.9% disease rate (vs Asymptomatic 25.4%).
    - Current smokers: 46.4% (vs never 25.2%).
    - Family history: 35.6% (vs 28.3% without).
    - Male: 35.4% vs Female: 24.7%.
    - Alcohol: weak (r ~ 0.04), far less important than smoking here.

7.4 Keep for future modeling: max_heart_rate_achieved, st_depression, age, ldl,
    hdl, resting_bp_systolic, exercise_minutes_per_week, hba1c, bmi,
    fasting_blood_sugar, plus categorical chest_pain_type, smoker_status,
    family_history, sex.
    Weakest: alcohol_units_per_week, sleep_hours, stress_score, triglycerides.
    Warning: the target is imbalanced (30.3% positive); a model predicting "no
    disease" always would be ~70% "accurate" while learning nothing, so use
    precision/recall or balanced metrics, not plain accuracy.

7.5 Final conclusion: heart disease in this dataset goes together with older age,
    a worse cholesterol profile, higher blood pressure, higher BMI, higher blood
    sugar, lower exercise capacity, smoking and certain chest-pain types. The data
    is clean, so the patterns are trustworthy. This analysis is a solid starting
    point, and the predictive modelling work is carried out in Part 8 of the same
    notebook (see modeling_report.txt for the full modeling write-up).
